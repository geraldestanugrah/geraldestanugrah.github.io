Landscape Fragmentation, Surface Moisture & Land Surface Temperature
Giam Siak Kecil–Bukit Batu

An integrated remote-sensing and spatial-analysis study examining how landscape structure and surface moisture relate to land surface temperature across the Giam Siak Kecil–Bukit Batu Biosphere Reserve.

01 / RESEARCH QUESTION

What is the relationship between landscape fragmentation, surface moisture, and land surface temperature?

The research examines spatial relationships between landscape configuration, surface moisture, and land surface temperature across the biosphere reserve. The analytical framework combines satellite-derived variables, landscape metrics, grid-based spatial integration, and non-parametric correlation analysis.
02 / DATA & METHODS

A reproducible workflow from satellite imagery to spatial interpretation.

  • Data acquisition: Landsat 9 imagery for 2025 through Google Earth Engine.
  • Land-cover classification: supervised Random Forest classification into seven land-cover classes, assessed using a confusion matrix, overall accuracy, and Kappa coefficient.
  • Biophysical indices: Normalized Difference Moisture Index (NDMI) and Land Surface Temperature (LST).
  • Landscape metrics: Patch Density (PD), Edge Density (ED), and Aggregation Index (AI).
  • Spatial integration: 500 × 500 m grid and zonal statistics, producing 9,692 valid analysis units.
  • Statistical analysis: Kolmogorov–Smirnov normality assessment and Spearman correlation in RStudio.
03 / KEY FINDINGS

Surface moisture showed the strongest bivariate relationship with LST among the tested indicators.

  • NDMI and LST: Spearman’s rho = −0.678.
  • Patch Density and LST: rho = +0.285.
  • Edge Density and LST: rho = +0.296.
  • Aggregation Index and LST: rho = −0.293.

These coefficients describe bivariate associations in the analysed grid cells. The project gallery now includes the corresponding Spearman scatterplots from the R analysis for visual interpretation. They do not establish causal effects. The buffer area is an additional analytical comparison area, not an official biosphere-reserve zone.

04 / MY CONTRIBUTION

Research design implementation, geospatial processing, statistical analysis, and WebGIS publication.

I carried out the satellite-image processing, land-cover classification workflow, NDMI and LST derivation, landscape-metric analysis, grid-based integration, correlation analysis, and WebGIS development. The WebGIS was built using Python geospatial libraries and Folium/Leaflet, with thematic layers and map controls for exploration.
05 / OUTPUT & LIMITATIONS

Maps, statistical evidence, and a public interactive spatial application.

The outputs include land-cover, NDMI, LST, PD, ED, AI, study-area maps, statistical summaries, and an interactive WebGIS. Interpretation is limited to the selected imagery, classification design, analytical grid, and tested variables; correlation results should not be read as causal explanations.
